## The Silent Signal  
  
The kitchen light hummed, a persistent, jagged sound that grated against Lena’s nerves. She stood by the counter, a plate of untouched pasta before her, watching the clock. 7:14 PM. Kai was supposed to be home at 6:30.  
  
When the front door finally clicked, the sound didn't bring relief; it brought a spike of adrenaline. Lena felt the familiar, frantic tightening in her chest—the urgent, pulsing need to know if he was coming toward her or moving away. She straightened her posture, smoothing the fabric of her skirt, readying herself to be seen, to be acknowledged.  
  
Kai entered the room with his keys already in his hand. He didn't look up, his gaze fixed on the screen of his phone, his thumb swiping with rhythmic, detached precision. He reached the kitchen island and set his bag down, his movements deliberate, creating a perimeter of silence around him.  
  
Hey, Lena said. The word was a bid, small and fragile, cast out into the room like a line into dark water.   
  
Kai glanced up, a flicker of something unreadable crossing his eyes before he looked back at his device. Hey. Long day.  
  
He walked past her toward the refrigerator. Lena felt her heart rate accelerate, the rhythm of his movement—the way he steered wide of her, the way he kept his eyes on the floor—triggering the old, deep-seated alarm. She needed to close the gap. She needed the physical proximity that usually grounded her, the sense that they were still a unit, still operating on the same frequency.  
  
I made the risotto, she said, her voice rising in pitch, a subconscious attempt to pull him back into the room. It’s still warm.  
  
Kai paused, his hand hovering over the handle of the fridge. He didn't turn. He felt the weight of her expectation like a physical pressure, a sudden, stifling density in the air. He wanted to sit on the couch, to stare at a blank wall, to let the static of his day drain out of his system in the quiet. The thought of having to calibrate himself to her intensity—to negotiate the space between them—felt like an impossible labor.  
  
I’m not really hungry, he said, his voice flat. I think I’m just going to head into the office for a bit. Catch up on some code.  
  
Lena took a step toward him, her hand half-raised, as if she could physically catch the air he was displacing. But you just got home. We haven't even… we haven't spoken all day.  
  
Kai finally turned, but his expression was guarded, his jaw set in a thin, neutral line. He looked at her, but he didn't see her; he saw a demand, a pull on his autonomy that he couldn't afford to meet.   
  
I just need a minute, Lena, he said, his tone measured, careful. I’m a little tapped out.  
  
He walked out of the kitchen, his footsteps fading into the hallway. Lena stood alone in the bright, humming room, the plate of food growing cold, the silence returning, heavy and absolute. She felt the familiar, chilling hollow in her stomach. She hadn't done anything wrong, yet she was already being left behind. She picked up her phone, her thumb hovering over the screen, ready to chase him, to demand the connection she felt slipping through her fingers. She didn't realize that in her need to reach him, she had already begun to push him further into the dark.
