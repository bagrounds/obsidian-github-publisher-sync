# The Unheld Weight: Part Three  
  
**Friday, 1:45 p.m.**  
  
🏗️ The atrium was not just a design; it was a physical manifestation of everything Eliza had ever believed about her work. 📏 The glass panels, the tension cables, the way the light would filter down through the central spine—it was a miracle of balance. 🧪 But as she sat in the conference room, surrounded by the project managers and the chief structural engineer, the blueprints in front of her looked like a foreign language.  
  
📉 Her brain was screaming for a resource that wasn't there. 🧠 She looked at the Chief Engineer, Mr. Henderson, who was waiting for her to justify the load-bearing capacity of the western wall. 🧱 Usually, when she hit this wall—the point where the math became so dense it felt suffocating—she would glance toward the corner of the room where David would sometimes sit in on the initial structural reviews. 🫂 Just the sight of his posture, the way he would lean back and offer a small, reassuring nod, would act as a circuit breaker for her anxiety. ⚡ It would pull her back into her prefrontal cortex, away from the fear of failure, and into the problem-solving logic.  
  
❌ He wasn't there. 🚫 The seat was empty.   
  
🫀 The physiological cascade was immediate. 🌊 Her vagal brake, that internal regulator that kept her heart steady under pressure, failed. 🥁 Her pulse hammered in her throat, a frantic, irregular rhythm that made it difficult to form a complete sentence. 🌫️ She felt a wave of nausea. 📉 The figures on the screen began to blur.  
  
💬 Henderson cleared his throat. 🗣️ Eliza, we’re looking for the specific adjustment for the wind load on the northwest corner. 🏗️ Are we good to proceed with the current tensile specifications?  
  
🧠 She opened her mouth, but the words felt trapped behind a wall of static. 🔨 She tried to conjure the memory of David’s voice, the calm, logical cadence of his reassurance, but the memory was cold, distant, and utterly ineffective. 🥀 It was like trying to fuel a furnace with shadows.  
  
📉 She felt the "social baseline" deficit with brutal, physical clarity. 🌪️ Without his presence to buffer the threat of the room, every set of eyes on her felt like a predator. 🌑 Every request for information felt like an attack. ⚖️ She wasn't just losing the argument; she was losing the ability to think.  
  
💬 I… she started, her voice sounding thin, fragile. 📉 She looked at her hands. ✍️ They were trembling. 🥀 She was an expert, she was a leader, she was the person who held the vision of the building together—and she was currently standing in the center of the room, completely and utterly unable to hold the weight of her own mind.  
  
💥 She stood up. 🚶‍♀️ The chair scraped harshly against the floor.   
  
💬 I need a moment, she whispered, the words barely audible over the sudden, ringing silence of the room.  
  
🚪 She didn't wait for a response. 🌫️ She walked out, the door clicking shut behind her, leaving the blueprints, the atrium, and the unheld weight of the entire project sitting, unsupported, on the table.
