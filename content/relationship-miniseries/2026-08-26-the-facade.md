# The Facade  
  
The morning sunlight in the kitchen was unforgiving, catching the fine layer of dust on the baseboards Maya had forgotten to scrub the night before. She stared at the sliver of grey, her pulse tightening. It was a small failure, a tiny breach in the fortress she built every morning before the coffee finished brewing.   
  
She stood by the island, smoothing her skirt, checking the digital clock for the third time in two minutes. Everything had to be calibrated. Her life was a graphic design of perfect proportions: career, home, marriage. As long as the alignment remained precise, she could suppress the gnawing sense that she was, at her center, an unfinished draft.  
  
Ethan entered the room, his presence a sudden, jarring shift in the atmosphere. He was wearing his favorite vintage tee, his hair a strategic mess, humming a song that sounded like a commercial jingle. He didn't notice the dust. He didn't notice the slight tremor in her hands. He just swung an arm around her shoulders, squeezing tight.  
  
Hey, superstar, he chirped, planting a kiss on her temple. Ready to conquer the world? Or at least the quarterly reports?  
  
Maya leaned into him, then pulled away just enough to straighten his collar. The armor was heavy today, pressing against her ribs. She needed to tell him about the Henderson account—the way her boss had pointed out the lack of cohesion in her latest presentation, the way she had felt her face burn as she realized he was right. She wanted to say, I feel like a fraud, Ethan. I feel like I’m running on fumes and I’m terrified I’m going to trip.  
  
But Ethan was already laughing at something he’d seen on his phone, his laughter bright and superficial, a polished surface that would slide right off anything heavy she tried to deposit on it.   
  
I’m good, she said, her voice sounding thin to her own ears. Just a busy day ahead.  
  
Ethan looked at her then, his eyes crinkling at the corners. He was the king of the quick fix, the master of the deflective joke. Don't let the man get you down, babe. You’re the best there is. If things get hairy, just remember: I’m the funny one, and you’re the smart one. That’s why it works.  
  
He wasn't trying to be cruel. He was trying to be the harbor, but he was building it with sand. Maya offered a tight, practiced smile, feeling the wall between them grow another inch higher. She didn't want to be the smart one. She wanted to be a person who could make mistakes without feeling like her entire identity was dissolving.   
  
She turned back to her coffee, the silence of the kitchen closing around her again. Beneath the mask of the competent, organized professional, her heart was beginning to race—a frantic, private rhythm that Ethan, in his cheerful, armored world, would never hear.
